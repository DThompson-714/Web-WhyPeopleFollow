# Worth Following: welcome email sequence (draft)

Three short emails that go out automatically when someone subscribes. Paste each one into
your newsletter service as an automated sequence (in Kit: **Automate → Sequences**).
Edit freely; these are drafts written from your own articles and About page.

Replace `https://www.whypeoplefollow.com` links if your domain changes.

---

## Email 1: sends immediately

**Subject:** I might be the worst manager ever
**Preview text:** And I hope you will be too.

Hi there,

Thanks for subscribing to **Worth Following**. I'm David Thompson, and for most of my 25 years leading IT teams, I proudly told anyone who would listen that I was the worst manager ever.

I meant it as a compliment.

I was an accidental manager. The job was offered to me, I took it for the raise, and I had no idea what I was doing. Then a senior leader told me they saw a lot of Emotional Intelligence in me. I said thank you, went back to my desk, and googled it.

That moment, and the Leadership Academy that followed, drew a clear line between managing people and leading them. I knew right away which side of that line I wanted to be on.

Here's what you can expect from me, every other Tuesday:

- **One honest story or lesson** from the room where it happens
- **One question** to sit with before your next team meeting
- **A real person on the other end.** Hit reply anytime. I read every message.

If you're new here, this is the best place to begin: [Start Here](https://www.whypeoplefollow.com/start-here)

Pull up a chair. I'm glad you're here.

David

*P.S. Quick question: what's the hardest part of leading your team right now? Just hit reply. I'd genuinely like to know.*

## Email 2: sends 3 days later

**Subject:** The plan didn't survive the first thirty seconds
**Preview text:** The worst HR moment of my career.

Hi again,

A few years ago, I had to let a good kid go. He had a great attitude, and he simply wasn't ready for the job. That happens.

I had a plan. Be honest. Be kind. Talk about what I genuinely liked about him, deliver the hard news gently, and offer to help with whatever came next. I told HR I wanted to lead the conversation. They agreed.

He walked into the conference room, and before I said a word, the HR representative stood up, patted him on the back and said, "I am so sorry about this."

He knew. I watched his shoulders drop. And it is very hard to bring someone back from that place once they've gone there.

What I took away from that day is something I still believe:

> **Process without emotional intelligence is just procedure.**

Nobody, regardless of the circumstances, should ever leave a room feeling like less of a person than when they walked in.

[Read the full story →](https://www.whypeoplefollow.com/articles/firing-an-employee-with-compassion)

David

## Email 3: sends 6 days after subscribing

**Subject:** One question before your next team meeting
**Preview text:** Would you follow you?

Hi,

Here's a question I want you to sit with this week:

**Think about the managers you've worked for. Which ones do you still call?**

I still call the leaders from my past when I need guidance. I've never once called a former manager who wasn't also a leader.

Now flip it. If your team members moved on tomorrow, would they call you?

If you're not sure, I built a two-minute reflection that might help. Nine honest statements about how you want to be remembered, how you handle your ego, and how much you trust your team. Nobody sees your answers but you.

[Take the "Would you follow you?" reflection →](https://www.whypeoplefollow.com/start-here#quiz)

Then hit reply and tell me how it went. Which area surprised you?

From here on, you'll hear from me every other Tuesday. Thanks for being someone who wants to be worth following.

David
