# The Worst Manager Ever's Field Guide (draft)

*Ten things I wish someone had told me my first year leading people.*
*By David Thompson, WhyPeopleFollow.com*

### 1. You become what you know, unless you decide not to.
Most of us manage the way we were managed: assign tasks, set deadlines, drive the staff. If you've never seen good leadership, you have to choose it on purpose.

### 2. When it goes wrong, it's on you. Every single time.
A manager tells upper management, "I explained the consequences to the staff." A leader says, "I'm sorry. This is on me." Take the blame out loud, then fix what you didn't give your team.

### 3. Process without emotional intelligence is just procedure.
Policies exist for good reasons. But how you treat someone on their worst day says everything about who you are.

### 4. Nobody should leave a room feeling smaller than when they walked in.
Before any hard conversation, decide how you want the person to feel walking out, then plan backward from there.

### 5. Hire people smarter than you, then let them prove it.
I suggested a technology stack. A new hire found the flaw in it within days, and he was right. My job was to say so.

### 6. Be wrong in public.
My team heard, at the lunch table, that my recommendation was the inferior one. Watching their leader change course without ego taught them more than any meeting could.

### 7. Check-ins don't have to be formal to be real.
Walk around. Eat lunch together. If the outcome is clear and the trust is there, don't schedule a meeting just to prove you're managing.

### 8. Good intentions still need good words.
I once had three solid reasons for a hard decision and couldn't get one of them out of my mouth clearly. Think it through before the conversation finds you.

### 9. Taking no risk is the bigger failure.
Teams that feel safe to make mistakes try unconventional ideas and find the breakthroughs. Sticking to the safe path isn't caution. It's fear.

### 10. Your real performance review is who still calls you.
Not the annual review. The people who show up to your retirement party, and the ones who still ask for your advice years later.
